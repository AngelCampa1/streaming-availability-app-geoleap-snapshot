# The complete streaming wars guide for 2026

**The streaming landscape in early 2026 is defined by aggressive price increases, mega-mergers reshaping ownership, and a maturing free tier ecosystem that now captures over 5% of total US TV viewing.** Netflix and HBO Max remain the prestige content leaders — tied at 30 Emmy wins apiece in 2025 — while Apple TV has emerged as the best value for ad-free 4K streaming at $12.99/month. Meanwhile, free services led by Tubi (100 million monthly users) have become a legitimate third pillar of television alongside paid streaming and traditional broadcast. What follows is a comprehensive matchup-by-matchup breakdown of every major service, including pricing across four countries, content libraries, technical specs, and the rapidly expanding world of free streaming.

---

## 1. Netflix vs HBO Max: the prestige heavyweight bout

Netflix and HBO Max (which cycled through a rebrand to "Max" in May 2023 before returning to the HBO Max name in July 2025) represent the two most culturally dominant streaming platforms. They tied at **30 Emmy wins** in 2025, though HBO Max earned 142 total nominations across just 23 programs versus Netflix's 120 nominations across 44 — reflecting HBO Max's higher per-show quality batting average.

### Pricing comparison

| Tier | Netflix (US) | HBO Max (US) |
|------|-------------|-------------|
| Ad-supported | $7.99/mo | $10.99/mo ($109.99/yr) |
| Standard (ad-free) | $17.99/mo | $18.49/mo ($184.99/yr) |
| Premium (4K) | $24.99/mo | $22.99/mo ($229.99/yr) |

Netflix is cheaper at the ad-supported level by **$3/month**, but HBO Max undercuts Netflix by $2/month at the premium 4K tier and offers annual billing discounts (~16% savings) that Netflix does not.

In the **UK**, Netflix runs £5.99–£18.99/month, while HBO Max launches on March 26, 2026, with four tiers from £4.99–£14.99/month — significantly cheaper than Netflix across every tier. In **Canada**, Netflix costs CAD $7.99–$23.99, but HBO Max remains unavailable (HBO content is licensed exclusively to Crave). In **Australia**, Netflix charges AU $9.99–$28.99 — the most expensive streamer in the market — while HBO Max launched in March 2025 at roughly AU $8.99–$22.

### Library and content

Netflix's US library stands at approximately **7,865 titles** (3,800 movies, 1,800 TV shows), with 62% being Netflix Originals. HBO Max offers roughly **35,000 hours** of premium content but fewer total titles, anchored by the HBO back catalog (The Sopranos, The Wire, Game of Thrones), Warner Bros. films, and Discovery+ reality content.

Netflix's standout 2024–2026 originals include *Adolescence* (8 Emmy wins), *Squid Game* Season 2, and Kathryn Bigelow's *A House of Dynamite*. HBO Max countered with *The Pitt* (Outstanding Drama Series Emmy), *The Penguin* (9 Emmys), *The White Lotus* Season 3, and *The Last of Us* Season 2. New Game of Thrones prequel *A Knight of the Seven Kingdoms* debuted in January 2026, with *Euphoria* Season 3 arriving in April 2026.

### Technical specs and ads

Both services lock **4K, HDR, Dolby Vision, and Dolby Atmos** behind their top-tier plans. Standard tiers on both cap at 1080p. Netflix allows 2–4 simultaneous streams depending on tier; HBO Max allows 2–4 with up to 100 downloads on Premium.

Netflix's ad tier runs **4–5 minutes of ads per hour** — the lightest among major streamers — though some titles remain unavailable on the ad plan. HBO Max's ad tier has crept up to **~6 minutes per hour**, a 50% increase from its original 4-minute promise at launch. Neither service allows ad skipping. Netflix operates in **190+ countries** (300M+ subscribers) versus HBO Max's **100+ countries** (128M subscribers), with HBO Max still expanding across Europe through early 2026.

## 2. Netflix vs Disney+: scale meets franchise power

Disney+ charges **$11.99/month** for its ad-supported Basic tier in the US and **$18.99/month** for ad-free Premium — pricing that now matches or exceeds Netflix's equivalent tiers. International pricing runs lower: £5.99–£14.99 in the UK, CAD $8.99–$16.99 in Canada, and AU $15.99–$20.99 in Australia (no ad tier in Australia or New Zealand).

Disney+'s library is substantially smaller at roughly **1,300–1,900 movies and 500–800 TV shows** in the US, but the franchise depth — Marvel, Star Wars, Pixar, National Geographic, 20th Century Studios — creates enormous draw. Internationally, Disney+ libraries are larger because they incorporate Hulu/Star content. Disney+ reports **~131.6 million paid subscribers** globally.

Disney+'s 2024–2026 highlights include *Andor* Season 2 (universally acclaimed), *Daredevil: Born Again*, *X-Men '97*, and the upcoming *Ahsoka* Season 2 and *The Mandalorian & Grogu* theatrical film. Netflix wins decisively on library breadth and global original output (597 originals released in 2025 alone); Disney+ wins on franchise loyalty and family content.

A key technical advantage for Disney+: both the Basic and Premium US tiers technically support **4K UHD**, though consistent 4K with Dolby Vision and Dolby Atmos is guaranteed only on Premium. Disney+ allows 4 simultaneous streams across all US tiers. Ad load sits at roughly **4–5 minutes per hour**, comparable to Netflix, with no ads on kids' profiles.

## 3. Disney+ vs Hulu: a merger in progress

The Disney+/Hulu comparison increasingly describes a single service rather than two competitors. Disney acquired Comcast's remaining 33% stake in Hulu for $8.6 billion in June 2025, and by August 2025 announced full integration of Hulu into the Disney+ app. As of March 2026, **all Hulu content is accessible through Disney+** for bundle subscribers, and the standalone Hulu app is being progressively shut down across devices. Full merger completion is expected by end of 2026.

Hulu remains **US-only** as a standalone subscription (though the "Hulu" brand replaced "Star" on Disney+ internationally in October 2025). Standalone Hulu pricing matches Disney+ at $11.99/month with ads and $18.99/month ad-free. The most compelling value proposition is the **Duo Basic bundle** at $12.99/month (both services with ads) and the **Trio Basic** at $16.99/month adding ESPN Select.

Hulu's library of roughly **4,000+ combined titles** complements Disney+'s family-focused catalog with next-day network TV (ABC, FX, FOX), mature originals, and critically acclaimed FX programming. *The Bear*, *Shōgun*, and *Only Murders in the Building* headline Hulu's 2024–2026 slate, alongside the upcoming *Malcolm in the Middle* revival and Ryan Murphy's *The Beauty*.

The most significant gap between the services is ad experience: Hulu carries **7–11 minutes of ads per hour** — the heaviest ad load among major paid streamers and nearly double Disney+'s ~4–5 minutes. Hulu's 4K library is also far smaller than Disney+'s, limited to select originals, and **Hulu does not support Dolby Atmos at all**.

## 4. Prime Video vs Apple TV: quantity versus quality, redefined

Amazon Prime Video and Apple TV (rebranded from Apple TV+ in October 2025) represent opposite philosophies. Prime Video offers the **largest library of any streamer** — roughly 20,000 movies and 3,600 TV shows — while Apple TV focuses exclusively on originals with only **~300+ titles**, supplemented by ~50 licensed films added in early 2026.

### The pricing inflection point

Amazon Prime Video costs $8.99/month standalone (with ads) or is included in the $14.99/month Prime membership. However, **a major change arrives April 10, 2026**: the ad-free add-on, previously $2.99/month, becomes "Prime Video Ultra" at **$4.99/month** (or $45.99/year), and critically, **4K streaming and Dolby Atmos are locked behind this new paywall**. Standard Prime members lose 4K access.

Apple TV charges a flat **$12.99/month** ($99.99/year) with no ads, **4K Dolby Vision and Dolby Atmos included for all subscribers**, and industry-leading 6 simultaneous streams. This makes Apple TV the **cheapest major ad-free 4K streaming service** — a Prime member wanting equivalent quality would pay $19.98/month total.

| Feature | Prime Video (post-April 2026) | Apple TV |
|---------|------------------------------|----------|
| Ad-free 4K price | $19.98/mo (Prime + Ultra) | $12.99/mo |
| Annual ad-free 4K | $184.99/yr | $99.99/yr |
| Simultaneous streams | 5 (Ultra) / 4 (standard) | 6 |
| Ads | 4–6 min/hr (doubled since launch) | None |
| Library size | 23,000+ titles | ~350 titles |

### Content and sports

Prime Video's hits include *Fallout* (65M viewers in 16 days), *The Boys* (final season April 2026), *Invincible*, *Reacher*, and *The Rings of Power*. Amazon also carries Thursday Night Football and a new 11-year NBA deal. Apple TV's roster punches well above its weight: *Severance* Season 2 was a cultural phenomenon, *Ted Lasso* Season 4 is confirmed for summer 2026, and the film *F1* earned **4 Academy Award nominations including Best Picture**. Apple added Formula 1 (all races) and MLS to its sports portfolio, included in the base subscription.

Prime Video operates in **240+ countries** (315M global viewers), while Apple TV covers **100+ countries** with a more consistent global library since it's primarily originals.

## 5. Paramount+ vs Peacock: mid-tier contenders in flux

Both services sit in a volatile position. The **Skydance-Paramount merger** closed in August 2025, creating Paramount Skydance Corporation, and David Ellison announced plans to merge Paramount+ and HBO Max into a single service following a pending WBD acquisition agreement. Meanwhile, Peacock remains under Comcast/NBCUniversal ownership with reports of potential Comcast-Paramount discussions.

### Pricing and availability

Paramount+ raised prices in January 2026 to **$8.99/month Essential** (with ads) and **$13.99/month Premium** (ad-free, includes Showtime content). International presence spans ~29+ countries plus SkyShowtime in additional European markets. Peacock charges **$7.99 (Select, limited library), $10.99 (Premium with ads), or $16.99 (Premium Plus, ad-free)** — but is available as a **full service only in the US**. Peacock's lack of international availability is its most significant strategic weakness.

Paramount+'s 79.1 million subscribers nearly double Peacock's ~41–44 million. Paramount+ holds exclusive UFC rights (7-year, $7.7B deal starting January 2026) and the Taylor Sheridan empire (*Landman*, *Tulsa King*, *Lioness*), plus the Star Trek franchise. Peacock counters with NBC's legendary library (*The Office*, *Parks and Recreation*), Bravo's reality content, and **the strongest US sports portfolio**: Sunday Night Football, NBA, Premier League (175+ matches), the 2026 Winter Olympics, and Super Bowl LX.

Paramount+ offers meaningfully better **4K/HDR/Dolby Atmos** support on its Premium tier, while Peacock's 4K library is limited and **does not support Dolby Atmos**. Both allow 3 simultaneous streams. Ad loads are comparable at roughly **4–5 minutes per hour** for both services' ad tiers.

## 6. Crunchyroll vs Funimation: there is no contest anymore

**Funimation permanently shut down on April 2, 2024.** Sony's Funimation Global Group completed its $1.175 billion acquisition of Crunchyroll in August 2021, and after a two-year migration, Funimation's app and website were decommissioned. Crunchyroll is now the **undisputed dominant anime streaming platform** globally.

As of February 2, 2026, Crunchyroll raised prices by $2 across all tiers: **Fan ($9.99/month), Mega Fan ($13.99/month), and Ultimate Fan ($17.99/month, US only)**. The free ad-supported tier was eliminated on December 31, 2025 — all content now requires a paid subscription, and all paid tiers are ad-free.

The library encompasses roughly **2,000 series and 50,000 episodes** across 200+ countries, with over 100 new simulcast titles per season. Crunchyroll holds exclusive streaming rights to **over 100 of the top 150 anime titles**. Standout 2024–2026 content includes *Solo Leveling*, *Dandadan*, *Jujutsu Kaisen* Season 3, and the theatrical release of *Demon Slayer: Infinity Castle* (5th highest-grossing film worldwide in 2025).

The transition was not seamless: approximately **192 Funimation titles** (60,000+ minutes) were never transferred to Crunchyroll, including notable series like *Steins;Gate* and *Angel Beats*. Users who owned Funimation digital copies lost access entirely. The platform's primary technical limitation remains **no 4K or HDR support** — maximum resolution is 1080p with stereo audio only, a notable gap when Netflix offers select anime in 4K HDR.

## 7. Tubi vs Pluto TV: the free streaming showdown

These two services represent fundamentally different viewing philosophies and together account for roughly **3.7% of all US TV viewing**.

**Tubi** (Fox Corporation) is the on-demand king with **52,000+ titles**, 100+ million monthly active users, and the lightest ad load in free streaming at **4–6 minutes per hour**. It requires no account to start watching (account needed only for R-rated content and personalization). The trade-off is video quality capped at **720p** for most content.

**Pluto TV** (Paramount Skydance) replicates the cable TV experience with **250–425 live linear channels** organized in a TV guide format, drawing from the Paramount/CBS/MTV/Nickelodeon ecosystem. Its on-demand library is significantly smaller (~2,000 titles), and the ad load on live channels runs a heavy **12–16 minutes per hour** — approaching traditional cable levels. No registration is needed.

| Factor | Tubi | Pluto TV |
|--------|------|----------|
| Best for | Active browsing, picking specific titles | Passive lean-back "what's on" viewing |
| Ad load | 4–6 min/hr | 12–16 min/hr (live) |
| On-demand library | 52,000+ titles | ~2,000 titles |
| Live channels | 260+ | 250–425 |
| Max resolution | 720p | 720p–1080p |
| Registration | Optional | Optional |

Many cord-cutters use both services since they serve complementary viewing modes and cost nothing.

## The full free streaming landscape in 2026

Free ad-supported streaming has exploded into a **$12 billion projected market by 2027**, with 69% of US households using at least one FAST service by late 2025. Here is every major option:

### US-based free services

**The Roku Channel** is the fastest-growing FAST service, hitting a record 3% of all US TV viewing in December 2025. It offers thousands of on-demand titles and **400+ live channels** with approximately **8 minutes of ads per hour**. A Roku account is required (free), and max resolution is 1080p. In August 2025, Roku launched "Howdy," a $2.99/month ad-free tier with 3,000+ titles.

**Plex** uniquely combines free streaming (50,000+ titles, 1,000+ live channels) with personal media server functionality. Ad load sits at roughly **5–7 minutes per hour**, and no account is needed to start watching. Resolution caps at 1080p. A Plex Pass subscription does not remove ads on free content — there is currently no ad-free option for Plex's streaming library.

**YouTube's free movie library** rotates roughly **500+ full-length films** at any time, with major studio participation from Warner Bros., Lionsgate, and Sony. Uniquely among free services, **some YouTube ads are skippable** (after 5 seconds), and roughly 35% of recent additions are available in **4K** — the highest resolution available on any free platform. No sign-in required.

**Amazon Freevee was discontinued in September 2025.** All content migrated to Prime Video's "Watch for Free" section, accessible with a free Amazon account (no Prime subscription needed). The Peacock free tier is **closed to new users since January 2023**, and Paramount+ has never offered a free tier (Pluto TV serves as Paramount's free play).

### Library-based services

**Kanopy** stands apart as a **completely ad-free** service accessible through 4,000+ participating public libraries and universities. Its 30,000+ title library is curated for quality, featuring exclusive partnerships with **The Criterion Collection, A24, and PBS**. Users typically receive 6–10 play credits per month with 72-hour viewing windows. Video quality reaches up to 1080p. The Kanopy Kids section offers unlimited viewing without consuming credits.

**Hoopla** provides instant access to over **2 million titles** across movies, TV, audiobooks, eBooks, comics, and music — all ad-free. Video quality is its weakest point, limited to **standard definition only** for movies and TV. Most libraries now allow 3–15 borrows per month (trending downward as libraries manage rising costs), with 72-hour loan periods for video content. The BingePass feature is notable: one borrow unlocks 7 days of access to entire collections from services like Curiosity Stream and Hallmark+.

### Country-specific public broadcaster services

**BBC iPlayer (UK)** delivers world-class content — *Doctor Who*, *Planet Earth*, *Peaky Blinders*, *Fleabag* — completely ad-free, funded by the £175/year TV licence. It is the only free service offering **4K UHD with HDR** on select programs, using the BBC-developed HLG format. Access requires a UK TV licence and is geo-restricted to the UK.

**ABC iview (Australia)** is the country's top-reaching broadcast VOD service with 3.4 million weekly viewers. Completely ad-free with no subscription required, it offers 5,000+ hours of Australian and British content including the global phenomenon *Bluey* (4.8 billion minutes watched on iview in 2025). Australia-only access, streaming quality up to approximately 1080p.

**ARD Mediathek and ZDF Mediathek (Germany)** are the twin pillars of Germany's public broadcasting streaming ecosystem, both largely ad-free and funded by the €18.36/month household broadcasting fee. ZDF relaunched as "Streaming Portal ZDF" in March 2025 with a modernized Netflix-style interface. Both primarily serve German-language content with limited English subtitles, geo-restricted mainly to Germany with partial EU access. ARD's combined catalog spans 700,000+ titles across all regional broadcasters. Both are planning enhanced coverage for the 2026 Winter Olympics and FIFA World Cup.

## What the data reveals about where streaming is headed

Three trends define the trajectory. First, **price convergence at the top**: ad-free tiers from Netflix, HBO Max, Disney+, and Hulu now cluster tightly between $17.99 and $18.99/month, making bundles and annual plans the primary differentiator. The Disney+/Hulu Duo Premium bundle at $19.99/month for two services represents the strongest value in paid streaming.

Second, **the ad-free premium is widening**. Amazon's decision to lock 4K behind a paywall starting April 2026, HBO Max's quiet 50% increase in ad load, and Crunchyroll's elimination of its free tier all point toward an industry systematically extracting more revenue from viewers who refuse ads. Apple TV remains the notable exception — ad-free 4K for all subscribers at $12.99/month.

Third, **free streaming has achieved critical mass**. Tubi, Pluto TV, and the Roku Channel combined now capture more US viewing than any single broadcast network. With Tubi's ad load at just 4–6 minutes per hour (versus cable's 13–16 minutes), the quality gap between free and paid ad tiers is narrowing fast — the primary trade-offs are video resolution (720p–1080p versus 4K) and content exclusivity rather than viewing experience. For viewers willing to sacrifice resolution and new originals, the free tier has never been more competitive.
