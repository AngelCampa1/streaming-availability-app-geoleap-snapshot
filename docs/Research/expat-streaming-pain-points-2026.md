# Expat Streaming Pain Points by Country Pair 2026

*Published March 2026*

---

## Overview

Moving abroad means losing access to the streaming content of your home country. This is not a minor inconvenience — for many expats, it affects daily entertainment habits, access to native-language news and culture, and the ability to watch major sporting events they have followed for years. This report examines the most common expat routes and the specific streaming problems each group faces.

---

## Most Common Expat Routes

Global migration patterns and international employment data point to a consistent set of high-volume expat corridors. These are the routes where streaming pain points are most commonly reported:

**UK → Australia / New Zealand**
One of the highest-volume English-speaking expat routes. British nationals moving to Australia or New Zealand represent one of the largest sources of demand for UK geo-unblocking services. The content gap is substantial — Australia has its own robust streaming ecosystem (Stan, Binge, Kayo, Foxtel, 9Now, 7Plus, ABC iview), but UK content is largely absent or delayed.

**US → United Kingdom / Europe**
American expats in Europe frequently report frustration with the reduced Netflix catalogue, the unavailability of US sports packages at reasonable prices, and the absence of US-specific streaming services. Hulu, Peacock, and US-specific ESPN+ content are entirely unavailable outside North America by default.

**US → Canada / Canada → US**
Despite geographic proximity and cultural overlap, the US and Canadian streaming markets are significantly different. US services unavailable in Canada (some Hulu content, NBC Peacock) and Canadian services unavailable in the US (Crave, some CBC content) create frustration for cross-border residents.

**India → UK / US / Australia**
The Indian diaspora is one of the largest globally. Indian expats moving to English-speaking countries frequently lose access to JioHotstar (IPL cricket, Indian Premier League, Bollywood), SonyLIV, and ZEE5 — or find they can only access them at much higher international pricing than they paid at home.

**UK → UAE / Middle East**
British expats in the UAE face a double challenge: VPN usage is restricted by UAE law, and BBC iPlayer — a major fixture of British culture — is geo-restricted. ITVX, All4, and My5 are similarly unavailable. The legal context makes workarounds more sensitive here than in most other expat destinations.

**Germany / France → Anglophone Countries**
European expats moving to the US, UK, or Australia lose access to German-language ARD Mediathek and ZDF Mediathek (public broadcasters with vast content archives) or French ARTE, France TV, and Canal+ content. These are free public broadcaster services at home, making the loss feel particularly unfair.

**Australia → UK / Europe**
Australians abroad lose access to ABC iview, SBS On Demand, 9Now, 7Plus, Kayo Sports, and Australian-exclusive content on Stan and Binge.

## What Content Expats Miss Most

Across all expat groups, certain content categories generate the strongest desire to maintain home-country access:

**Sports (highest priority)**
Local sports coverage is the single biggest driver. British expats want Premier League, Formula 1, cricket via Sky/TNT Sports, and BBC/ITV sports coverage. American expats want NFL Sunday Ticket, NBA League Pass at US prices, and MLB.tv. Australian expats want cricket (NRL, AFL, BBL) via Foxtel/Kayo and Seven Network sports. Indian expats want IPL and international cricket via JioHotstar.

**News and current affairs**
BBC News, ITV News, and Channel 4 News are culturally important to British expats. Americans often want CNN, NBC News, or local US affiliates. The ability to watch live news from home — not just read text online — matters to many expats.

**Soap operas and long-running dramas**
EastEnders, Coronation Street, Emmerdale, and Hollyoaks have dedicated audiences who watch daily. Losing access to ongoing serial content is particularly disruptive because catching up later becomes impractical as episodes accumulate.

**Free-to-air catch-up TV**
BBC iPlayer, ITVX, All4, and My5 are habits, not luxuries, for British people. They are free at home, included in the TV licence, and represent a significant portion of British TV watching. Finding them unavailable abroad — while still having access to UK news about the shows — creates specific frustration.

**Late-night comedy and topical content**
Content like BBC Question Time, Have I Got News For You, or US shows like Saturday Night Live that comment on current events are most valuable when watched in near-real-time. Delayed access often makes them irrelevant.

## Cost of Replacing Home Streaming Abroad

When expats try to legitimately replace lost home-country streaming, the costs can be substantial:

**British expat in Australia trying to replicate UK streaming:**
- Amazon Prime Video UK: only accessible from UK-registered account
- BBC iPlayer: geo-restricted to UK, free in UK
- Sky/TNT Sports for Premier League: £40–50/month in UK equivalent; Australian alternatives don't cover the same matches
- Estimated cost of legal alternatives: £50–100/month, with content gaps remaining

**American expat in Europe trying to replicate US streaming:**
- Hulu: not available outside North America at any price
- Peacock: not available outside North America
- US Netflix: accessible but only at the local tier's library
- ESPN+: not available outside the US
- YouTube TV / Sling: geofenced to the US
- Estimated cost of legal alternatives: incomplete; some content simply unavailable

**Indian expat in the UK trying to replicate home streaming:**
- JioHotstar International: available at premium international pricing, approximately $10–15/month vs effectively subsidised pricing in India
- ZEE5 International: available at £7–12/month
- SonyLIV: available at premium pricing internationally
- Estimated additional monthly cost vs India: £20–35/month

## Conclusion

Expat streaming pain is real, measurable, and affects content categories — sports, news, and free public-broadcaster catch-up TV — that are central to cultural identity for many people. Smart DNS represents the most practical and cost-effective solution for expats who want to maintain access to their home country's streaming ecosystem without sacrificing device compatibility or streaming quality.
