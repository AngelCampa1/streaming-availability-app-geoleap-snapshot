# Sports Blackout Rules Explained 2026

*Published March 2026*

---

## What Are Blackout Rules

Blackout rules are restrictions applied to live sports broadcasts that prevent viewers in specific geographic areas from watching certain games or matches via streaming or cable, even if they have purchased the relevant subscription or streaming package. The blacked-out game is typically available via a different broadcaster (usually a local or national broadcast channel) or in person at the venue.

Blackout rules are a legacy of agreements between sports leagues, broadcast networks, and team ownership. They were originally designed to protect stadium attendance and local broadcast deals — the theory being that if a game is available for free on local TV or easy to stream, fans in the local market will stay home rather than buy a ticket, and local TV affiliates will lose their exclusive audience.

In practice, blackout rules frustrate fans and are widely regarded as an anachronism in the streaming era. Many leagues have relaxed or eliminated them over the past decade, but several significant blackout situations persist.

---

## Premier League: The 3pm Saturday Blackout

The most famous blackout rule in world football is the Premier League's 3pm Saturday blackout in the United Kingdom.

**What it is:** No Premier League match kicking off at 3pm on a Saturday may be broadcast live on UK television or via any UK streaming service. This applies to Sky Sports, TNT Sports, Amazon Prime Video, and any other rights holder operating in the UK.

**Why it exists:** The blackout was established to protect lower-league football (the Football League, now the EFL) from the commercial dominance of the Premier League. The fear was that if Premier League clubs were all visible on TV at the traditional Saturday afternoon slot, grassroots and lower-league attendance would collapse. The Football League clubs retain significant political influence within British football governance, and the 3pm blackout is protected by them.

**The scope of the blackout:** Every single 3pm Saturday kick-off in the Premier League, across all 20 clubs and every matchday of the season, is blacked out. This represents roughly 40–50 matches per season that UK viewers cannot watch live on any platform.

**What viewers can do:** Listen via BBC Radio 5 Live, follow text commentary, and watch highlights on Match of the Day (BBC) after 10:30pm. Viewers with Smart DNS configured to access streaming from another country can watch the matches via international rights holders — Sky Sports is available in international markets, and beIN Sports, ESPN+, and other platforms carry Premier League matches without the 3pm blackout restriction.

**Supporters of the blackout** argue it has protected lower-league football. Attendances at Championship and League One level, while affected by many factors, have not collapsed in the decades since the blackout was introduced.

**Critics of the blackout** argue that international fans watching via illegal streams are already watching these games; the only people truly blocked are UK-based fans who want to watch legally.

## NFL: Local Market Blackouts

The NFL historically had one of sports' most aggressive blackout regimes. The original rule blacked out home games in the local market if the stadium was not sold out 72 hours before kickoff.

**Current status (2026):** The NFL suspended its home blackout rule in 2015 and has not reinstated it. NFL games are now shown in all markets regardless of stadium sellout status. This is a significant departure from the historical position.

**What remains:** Out-of-market blackouts. NFL Sunday Ticket (now on YouTube TV and YouTube Primetime Channels in the US) provides access to out-of-market games, but in-market games remain limited to the local broadcast network. In the US, a fan in New York cannot use Sunday Ticket to watch the Giants or Jets play at home — those games are only on local CBS/Fox affiliates, not Sunday Ticket.

**International context:** NFL Game Pass International (available outside the US) has provided full live coverage of every game. This product is not available to US subscribers.

## NBA League Pass Blackouts

NBA League Pass is the league's direct-to-consumer streaming product, offering access to out-of-market games. Local market blackouts are a standard feature.

**How NBA blackouts work:** If you subscribe to League Pass in the United States, games involving your local market team are blacked out. A League Pass subscriber in Los Angeles cannot use League Pass to watch Lakers or Clippers games — those are only available on the team's regional sports network (RSN) or on national broadcast if the game is selected for national TV.

**National broadcast blackouts:** Games selected for national TV broadcasts (ABC, ESPN, TNT, NBC Sports) are also blacked out on League Pass during the live window. They are typically available on demand after 3 hours.

**International League Pass:** International subscribers outside the US have fewer blackout restrictions. All games are generally available.

**The RSN problem:** Regional Sports Networks, which traditionally held local NBA rights, have been in significant financial distress since 2023–2025 following the bankruptcy of Diamond Sports Group (Bally Sports). This has left some NBA markets without a functioning local broadcast deal, creating situations where games are technically blacked out on League Pass but also unavailable on the blacked-out regional broadcaster.

## NHL Blackouts

The NHL uses a similar out-of-market blackout model via NHL.tv (now integrated into ESPN+/Hulu/Max sports packages in the US, and available directly internationally).

**Local blackouts:** Games in your local market team's territory are blacked out. In-market games require a regional sports network subscription.

**National broadcast blackouts:** Games on national TV (ABC, ESPN, TNT, TBS) are blacked out during the live window.

**Canadian blackouts:** Canadian subscribers face extensive blackouts because most markets have at least one NHL team, and national broadcasters (CBC, Sportsnet, TVA Sports) hold broad rights. A Canadian NHL.tv subscriber may find that more games are blacked out than are available.

## MLS Blackouts

MLS Season Pass on Apple TV+ has simplified the broadcast rights picture for Major League Soccer significantly. Apple holds global MLS rights, which means:

- No regional sports network splits
- In-market blackouts are minimal compared to historical norms
- Subscribers can watch every game on the same platform

Some specific matches (Leagues Cup, US Open Cup) have separate rights situations and may not be included.

## Why Blackouts Exist: Protecting Stadium Attendance

The original economic logic of blackout rules rests on the assumption that local TV availability reduces stadium attendance. This was a reasonable concern in the 1970s and 1980s when:

- Ticket revenue was a primary income source for franchises
- Local TV deals were structured around exclusivity
- Audiences had limited viewing options

By the 2020s, stadium attendance as a percentage of team revenue has declined relative to media rights income. The NFL, NBA, NHL, and Premier League all earn more from their broadcast and streaming deals than from ticket sales across their respective team bases. The economic rationale for blackouts protecting ticket revenue is much weaker than it was when these rules were created.

Sports leagues and franchises increasingly recognise that maximising legal viewership — including local viewing — builds fandom that translates to merchandise, licensing, and next-generation fan acquisition. The trend is toward fewer, not more, blackout restrictions.

## How Smart DNS Helps Fans Watch Blacked-Out Games

Smart DNS allows a viewer to appear to be in a different geographic location for the purposes of streaming platform authentication. This has two relevant applications for blackout rules:

**Accessing international rights holders:** For the Premier League 3pm blackout in the UK, a viewer using Smart DNS configured to appear in another country (e.g., the US or Australia) can access the game via ESPN+ or Optus Sport, which do not apply the UK 3pm blackout. The match is being legally broadcast in those territories; the viewer is accessing a legal stream.

**Bypassing in-market blackouts:** A US viewer near a major city using Smart DNS to appear in a different US market can potentially access League Pass games that are blacked out in their actual location. This is a terms-of-service issue rather than a legal one.

The legal and terms-of-service distinction matters. Accessing an international rights holder's legitimate broadcast of a game (Premier League 3pm blackout via ESPN+) occupies different territory from spoofing your US location to evade domestic in-market blackouts.
