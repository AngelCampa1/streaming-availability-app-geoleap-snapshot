# Streaming Legal Landscape by Country 2026

*Published March 2026*

---

## Overview of International Streaming Law

The legal landscape surrounding streaming geo-restriction, geo-unblocking, and the tools used to circumvent geographic content restrictions is genuinely complex and varies substantially by country. The good news for most users in most Western democracies is that personal use of geo-unblocking tools is not a criminal matter. The legal concerns that do exist are primarily in the domain of civil contract law (Terms of Service) and, in a minority of countries, specific telecommunications regulations.

This document examines the legal status of geo-unblocking by country, distinguishes between Terms of Service violations and legal violations, and summarises what streaming platforms can and cannot legally enforce.

---

## Country-by-Country Legal Status

### United States

VPNs and Smart DNS services are legal in the United States. There is no federal law prohibiting their use for personal purposes, including accessing foreign streaming content.

The relevant legal framework is civil rather than criminal. The Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (CFAA) was interpreted in some early cases as potentially applying to ToS violations, but the Supreme Court's ruling in Van Buren v. United States (2021) significantly narrowed its scope. Accessing a streaming service from the wrong geographic location via Smart DNS is not CFAA territory.

A streaming platform could theoretically sue a user for breach of contract for violating ToS, but no such case has been brought against an individual consumer in the US context. The practical and reputational costs of suing customers for watching TV are prohibitive for platforms.

**Summary: Legal for personal use. ToS issue only.**

### United Kingdom

VPNs and Smart DNS services are legal in the United Kingdom. The Computer Misuse Act does not apply to ToS violations for accessing geo-restricted content.

The UK's Intellectual Property Office has historically taken the position that geo-unblocking to access legitimately available content (content that is simply restricted by geography, not pirated) does not constitute copyright infringement. The key issue is that the content itself is not being copied — it is being streamed.

Post-Brexit, the UK departed from EU frameworks that had briefly required streaming services to provide EU-wide portability (the EU Portability Regulation). UK residents no longer have a legal right to access their home streaming subscriptions when travelling in Europe.

**Summary: Legal for personal use. ToS issue only.**

### European Union

The EU presents a nuanced picture. Within the EU, the Portability Regulation (effective 2018) requires streaming platforms to allow subscribers to access their home-country subscription when temporarily visiting another EU member state. This means an EU resident travelling within the EU has a legal right to their home library — portability is legally mandated.

However, the Portability Regulation does not require platforms to allow access to other EU countries' libraries. A German subscriber using Smart DNS to access French Netflix, or a French subscriber accessing UK streaming services (post-Brexit), is still in ToS territory rather than under legal protection.

Individual EU member states have generally not passed laws restricting VPN or Smart DNS use for streaming. Germany, France, the Netherlands, Sweden, Spain, and other major markets treat it as a civil ToS matter.

**Summary: Legal across EU. EU portability gives specific rights within EU for temporary travel. Cross-border access is ToS issue.**

### Australia

VPNs and Smart DNS services are legal in Australia for personal use. In 2016, the Australian Competition and Consumer Commission (ACCC) took the position that using a VPN to access foreign streaming services was not illegal and that Netflix's attempt to enforce against VPN users was a commercial, not legal, matter.

Australia has implemented a regime (via the Copyright Amendment (Online Infringement) Act 2015 and subsequent amendments) that allows rights holders to apply to courts to block piracy sites. This regime targets sites that facilitate copyright infringement (piracy) — not tools like VPNs or Smart DNS used to access legitimately licensed content.

**Summary: Legal for personal use. ToS issue only.**

### Canada

VPNs and Smart DNS services are legal in Canada. The relevant legislation (Copyright Act, Criminal Code) does not address personal geo-unblocking. Canada has its own complex streaming regulation landscape (CRTC), but these regulations govern platforms, not users.

**Summary: Legal for personal use. ToS issue only.**

### New Zealand

New Zealand has not enacted specific legislation addressing VPN or Smart DNS use for streaming. The position is similar to Australia — it is a civil ToS matter, not a legal one.

**Summary: Legal for personal use. ToS issue only.**

### Germany

Germany has strict copyright law, but VPN and Smart DNS use for streaming geo-unblocking is not a copyright infringement matter — it is a ToS matter. German courts have not found consumers liable for accessing foreign streaming content via geo-unblocking tools. Germany's copyright law focuses on reproduction and distribution, not streaming.

**Summary: Legal for personal use. ToS issue only.**

### Japan

VPNs are legal in Japan. Smart DNS for streaming is not specifically addressed in Japanese law. Personal use for accessing foreign streaming content is not a criminal matter.

**Summary: Legal for personal use. ToS issue only.**

### Brazil

VPNs are legal in Brazil. Marco Civil da Internet (Brazil's internet civil rights law) does not prohibit VPN or Smart DNS use. Accessing foreign streaming content is a ToS matter.

**Summary: Legal for personal use. ToS issue only.**

### India

VPNs are in a changing regulatory environment in India. In 2022, the Indian Computer Emergency Response Team (CERT-In) issued rules requiring VPN providers operating in India to maintain user logs for five years and share them with authorities on request. Several major VPN providers removed their servers from India in response.

These rules affect VPN service providers operating in India, not individual users of VPN services. Using a VPN as an individual consumer for geo-unblocking streaming is not illegal in India. However, the policy direction warrants monitoring.

**Summary: Individual use not illegal. Regulatory environment around providers is tightening.**

### United Arab Emirates

The UAE presents a more restricted environment. The UAE Telecom Regulatory Authority (TRA) restricts VPN use, but the regulations are nuanced. VPN use is permitted for legitimate business purposes. Personal use of VPNs to access blocked VoIP services (Skype, WhatsApp calls) is explicitly prohibited and can result in fines.

Geo-unblocking streaming content occupies a grey area. Enforcement has historically focused on VoIP circumvention and content deemed harmful under UAE law, not on individuals accessing foreign Netflix libraries. However, the legal risk is meaningfully higher in the UAE than in Western markets.

Smart DNS presents a different risk profile from VPN in the UAE — it does not encrypt traffic and does not provide the full bypass capabilities of a VPN. Its use for streaming geo-unblocking is lower profile.

**Summary: VPN restriction laws exist. Personal streaming geo-unblocking is grey area. Consult local legal advice. Smart DNS is lower risk than VPN.**

## Terms of Service vs Legal Issues

The most important distinction in this area is between a Terms of Service violation and a legal violation.

**Terms of Service violations:**
- Governed by the contract between you and the streaming platform
- Consequences: account suspension or termination, at the platform's discretion
- No criminal liability
- Very rarely enforced against individual consumers
- Netflix, Disney+, and other platforms have ToS provisions against geo-circumvention but have not pursued individual user enforcement actions

**Legal violations:**
- Governed by law, not contract
- Consequences: potential civil or criminal liability depending on jurisdiction
- Relevant in the UAE (VPN restrictions) and China, Russia, Iran, North Korea (discussed in the companion VPN bans document)
- Not applicable to individual streaming consumers in Western democracies

The confusion between these categories is deliberate in some platform communications and inadvertent in others. When Netflix says you "may not" use a VPN in its Terms of Service, it is describing a contractual restriction, not a legal prohibition. The legal context is entirely different.

## What Platforms Can and Cannot Enforce

**What platforms can do:**
- Detect and block IP addresses associated with VPNs and proxy services
- Suspend or terminate accounts that appear to be using geo-circumvention consistently
- Refuse service or require additional verification in certain countries
- Update ToS to strengthen contractual grounds for enforcement
- Apply geo-IP blocking to restrict access by location

**What platforms cannot do (in most jurisdictions):**
- Sue individual consumers for criminal copyright infringement for streaming via geo-circumvention (because no reproduction occurs; streaming is ephemeral)
- Demand compensation from individual consumers in most markets
- Access your device or data beyond what your use of their service involves
- Prevent you from using Smart DNS at the network level (beyond IP blocking at the application level)

**The detection arms race:**
Platforms invest continuously in better detection of VPN and proxy traffic. Datacenter IP ranges used by VPN providers are frequently identified and blocked. Smart DNS services that route through residential or ISP-grade IP ranges are harder to detect and have longer operational windows before detection.

## Smart DNS Legal Considerations

Smart DNS is a different technical category from VPN in several respects that are legally relevant:

1. **No traffic encryption:** Smart DNS does not encrypt your traffic. This means it is not a "circumvention device" in the technical sense used by some laws targeting encryption tools.

2. **DNS-level operation:** Smart DNS operates at the domain name resolution level, not at the IP routing level. This is a standard network configuration change (changing your DNS server) that users make legitimately for many reasons.

3. **Lower regulatory profile:** In countries where VPN regulation exists (UAE, India), Smart DNS is less frequently addressed in specific regulation.

4. **ISP detection:** Smart DNS is less visible to ISPs than VPN because there is no encrypted tunnel. Your ISP can see your traffic is going to a streaming service.

## Consumer Rights in Different Regions

**EU consumers** have the strongest formal rights — the Portability Regulation gives them explicit access to their home library when travelling within the EU.

**UK consumers** had portability under the EU framework but lost it after Brexit. Lobbying for a UK portability framework has not resulted in legislation as of 2026.

**Australian consumers** have benefited from ACCC's consumer-friendly interpretation of geo-unblocking as not unlawful.

**US consumers** have broad de facto freedom but no specific statutory protections.

**Consumers in restricted jurisdictions** (UAE, China, Russia) have fewer protections and higher risk, with varying enforcement levels.

---

## Conclusion

For the vast majority of GeoLeap's target users — expats and travellers in Western democracies — geo-unblocking streaming content is not a legal issue. It is a contract matter between the user and the streaming platform. Platforms can and do attempt to detect and block geo-circumvention, but they do not pursue individual consumers through legal channels. Smart DNS, specifically, is technically distinct from VPN in ways that reduce its regulatory profile even in markets with VPN-related rules.
