# VPN Banned Countries: Streaming in China, Russia, UAE 2026

*Published March 2026*

---

## Countries with VPN Restrictions

Before examining each country's specific situation, it is important to distinguish between different categories of VPN restriction:

**Outright ban with active enforcement:** North Korea, Turkmenistan, Belarus (de facto). VPNs are prohibited and the internet is so restricted that they are largely irrelevant to streaming.

**Heavily restricted with licensing system:** China. Only government-approved VPNs are permitted. Unapproved VPN use is technically illegal and subject to enforcement for companies and more occasionally for individuals.

**Restricted with specific prohibitions:** Iran, Russia. VPN services must register with government authorities. Specific uses (accessing banned services) are restricted. Personal enforcement is inconsistent.

**Restricted for specific uses:** UAE. VPN use for legitimate business is permitted. Using VPNs to access blocked VoIP services is specifically prohibited and fined.

**Not restricted:** The vast majority of countries, including all Western democracies, have no VPN restrictions for personal use.

This distinction matters significantly. A traveller in the UAE faces a different legal context from a traveller in China, and both are different from a traveller in Germany or Australia.

## China: The Great Firewall

### VPN Legal Status

China maintains the most sophisticated internet filtering system in the world, known colloquially as the Great Firewall. The system blocks thousands of foreign websites and services, including Google, Facebook, YouTube, Instagram, Twitter/X, and the majority of Western streaming platforms.

VPN use in China is technically illegal without government authorisation. The Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (MIIT) regulates VPN services, and only VPNs operating through licensed, government-sanctioned channels are permitted. These approved VPNs are designed for corporate use and route through monitored infrastructure.

Unapproved VPN use has historically been treated differently for foreign nationals and Chinese citizens. Foreign business travellers have generally been tolerated — enforcement has focused on services that sell unapproved VPNs domestically, not on individual users. However, this is not a legal protection; it is an enforcement priority.

Individual enforcement against foreign tourists and short-term visitors is rare. Individual enforcement against Chinese nationals is more active, particularly for people involved in distributing or promoting unapproved VPN services.

The Great Firewall's technical capabilities have improved substantially. Protocol obfuscation techniques used by VPN providers are increasingly detected and blocked. VPN reliability in China is variable and tends to degrade around politically sensitive periods.

### What Streaming Is Available in China

China's domestic streaming market is substantial and features world-class platforms. Services that are legally available in China include:

**iQIYI (爱奇艺):** One of China's largest streaming platforms, owned by Baidu. Extensive library of Chinese drama, film, anime, and some licensed international content. Known for high-production-value original series. Has attempted limited international expansion.

**Youku (优酷):** Owned by Alibaba. Another major streaming platform with broad Chinese content.

**Tencent Video (腾讯视频):** Owned by Tencent. One of the three major Chinese streaming platforms alongside iQIYI and Youku. Extensive Chinese content and some licensed international productions.

**Bilibili (哔哩哔哩):** Originally an animation, comics, and gaming (ACG) platform, now a broad video platform with a young audience. Strong in anime (significant licensed Japanese anime catalogue).

**CCTV and state broadcaster content:** China Central Television and provincial broadcasters have online streaming presence.

### Unavailable in China

The following are blocked or unavailable without circumvention:
- Netflix, Disney+, Amazon Prime Video, Apple TV+
- YouTube, Twitch
- All major Western social video platforms
- Most international news streaming

Netflix does not have a licensed presence in China, a deliberate business decision that has persisted despite occasional reports of negotiations. The Chinese film content quota system and censorship requirements have made a licensed Netflix launch in China structurally difficult.

## Russia: Tightening Restrictions

### VPN Legal Status

Russia has progressively tightened VPN regulation since 2017, when the Federal Law on Information, Information Technologies and the Protection of Information was amended to require VPN services to register with Roskomnadzor (Russia's federal communications regulator) and to block access to websites on the government's blacklist.

In practice, many international VPN providers refused to comply with these requirements and either blocked Russian users from their services or removed Russian servers. VPN use itself was not criminalised for individuals; the law targeted the services rather than the users.

Since Russia's invasion of Ukraine in February 2022 and subsequent tightening of internet regulation, the environment has changed significantly. Russia has blocked or restricted many Western internet services, including Facebook, Instagram, and many news sites. Roskomnadzor has increased blocking of VPN services that enable access to banned content.

Individual use of blocked VPN services is in a grey legal area — technically violating provisions of Russian telecommunications law, but individual enforcement is not consistent. Russians have continued to use VPNs at scale; demand for VPN services spiked sharply in Russia following the Ukraine invasion.

### What Streaming Is Available in Russia

The departure of Western streaming platforms following the Ukraine invasion changed the Russian streaming landscape significantly:

**Netflix:** Suspended operations in Russia in March 2022. Russian subscribers' access was cut off.

**Disney+:** Never launched in Russia.

**Domestic alternatives:**
- **Kinopoisk:** Owned by Yandex, one of Russia's dominant streaming platforms. Extensive Russian and Soviet film archive, original series, and licensed content.
- **IVI:** Major Russian subscription and AVOD streaming platform. Large library of Russian and international (licensed where available) content.
- **Okko:** Another major Russian streaming service with a broad catalogue.
- **START:** Premium Russian platform with originals.

The departure of Netflix and Western platforms opened space for domestic services but left Russian audiences without access to current Hollywood output.

## Iran

### VPN Legal Status

Iran has extensive internet filtering (referred to domestically as the "National Information Network" or NIN project). Thousands of websites are blocked, including Google, YouTube, WhatsApp, Telegram (periodically), and major social media platforms.

VPN use is technically illegal in Iran, but enforcement targets providers and distribution of VPN tools rather than individual users consistently. VPN usage is very widespread among Iranian internet users — estimates suggest a majority of Iranian internet users have used circumvention tools. This mass use makes individual enforcement impractical.

Access to VPNs and circumvention tools spiked dramatically during the 2022 protests following the death of Mahsa Amini, during which Iran imposed internet shutdowns and restrictions that made circumvention tools politically significant.

### Streaming Available in Iran

Domestic streaming platforms operate under strict content filtering requirements. International platforms are blocked. The domestic streaming ecosystem includes Persian-language content services, but the catalogue of international content available through legal channels is very limited.

## North Korea: Total Restriction

North Korea's internet situation is in a different category entirely. Ordinary North Korean citizens do not have access to the international internet. A small elite have supervised access. Streaming as a concept applicable to the consumer market does not exist.

## UAE: Nuanced Restrictions

### VPN Legal Status

The UAE's approach to VPNs is more targeted than China or Russia's. The UAE Telecommunications and Digital Government Regulatory Authority (TDRA) permits VPN use for legitimate business purposes. The specific prohibitions are:

- Using VPNs to access VoIP services (Skype, WhatsApp voice calls, FaceTime) that compete with UAE telecoms — this is explicitly prohibited
- Accessing content banned under UAE law

Using a VPN to access a foreign Netflix library, BBC iPlayer, or other geo-restricted streaming content is in a grey area. The law does not specifically prohibit streaming geo-unblocking, and enforcement has not targeted streaming users. However, operating under UAE law with any circumvention tool carries more risk than in Western markets.

### What Streaming Is Available in the UAE

The UAE has a robust legal streaming ecosystem:
- Netflix, Disney+, Amazon Prime Video, and Apple TV+ all operate in the UAE with local Arabic content alongside international libraries
- OSN+ (formerly part of beIN/OSN) carries major Western content
- Shahid (Arabic streaming platform) is a major regional platform
- beIN Sports covers major sports

The UAE's streaming library is generally strong for international content, with some gaps around content that does not pass UAE content moderation (certain LGBTQ+ content, politically sensitive material).

## Smart DNS as Alternative Approach

Smart DNS has a different technical and legal profile from VPN in the restricted-country context:

**Lower detection profile:** Smart DNS does not create an encrypted tunnel. Great Firewall and similar DPI (deep packet inspection) systems that detect VPN protocols may not flag Smart DNS traffic as circumvention.

**No traffic encryption:** In China and similar environments, Smart DNS traffic is visible to network operators. This has privacy implications but may reduce its profile as "circumvention."

**Effective for streaming geo-restriction:** For the specific use case of making a streaming platform's authentication check see a different country, Smart DNS works effectively.

**Not effective for accessing fully blocked services:** If Netflix's website and apps are completely blocked (as in China), Smart DNS alone does not unblock them — that requires changing your apparent IP address, not just DNS. A full solution in China requires VPN-level tools, which carry higher legal risk.

## What Travellers Need to Know

For travellers visiting restricted countries from Western markets, the practical guidance is:

**Before you travel:**
- Download streaming apps and content for offline viewing before departure
- Netflix, Disney+, and Prime Video all have offline download features
- Download enough content for your trip duration to reduce reliance on local streaming

**In China:**
- VPN reliability is variable; pre-install and test before travel
- Some hotel Wi-Fi networks have VPN-like corporate access tools that legally provide foreign internet access
- Smart DNS may work for streaming but does not unblock Chinese-blocked services fully
- Legal risk for short-term foreign visitors is low, but use discretion

**In the UAE:**
- Legal streaming options (Netflix, Disney+, Prime Video) are available and work well
- Use legal services wherever possible
- Avoid VPN use for VoIP; this is the primary enforcement focus
- Smart DNS for streaming geo-unblocking is low-profile

**In Russia:**
- Access to Western streaming platforms may be problematic regardless of circumvention tools, as many have suspended service
- Focus on downloaded content and legal domestic alternatives if visiting

## Conclusion

The countries where streaming and VPN use are genuinely restricted are a small minority of the world. China is the most technically sophisticated and legally clear case of VPN restriction. Russia's environment has tightened significantly since 2022. The UAE has specific restrictions on VoIP circumvention that are actively enforced but is generally streaming-friendly. For travellers and expats in these markets, Smart DNS presents a lower-profile alternative to VPN for streaming geo-restriction, though it does not solve the broader access problem in markets like China where services are fully blocked.
