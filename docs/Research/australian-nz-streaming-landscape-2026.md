# Australian & New Zealand Streaming Landscape 2026

*Published March 2026*

---

## Market Overview

Australia and New Zealand share cultural and linguistic ties but have developed distinct streaming ecosystems. Australia hosts a mature market with strong free-to-air (FTA) broadcaster catch-up services, a competitive subscription VOD landscape, and a dominant pay sports platform. New Zealand's market is smaller but has its own set of free-to-air catch-up services alongside Australian services that partially extend across the Tasman.

Combined, both markets represent a distinctive streaming environment that differs meaningfully from the UK, US, or European models. The prominence of free ad-supported streaming (FAST) through public broadcaster apps is higher than in most comparable markets, and sports streaming is concentrated in ways that create significant pain for fans who travel or move abroad.

## Australian Streaming Landscape

### Stan

Stan is Australia's largest domestic subscription VOD platform not owned by a broadcast network. Owned by Nine Entertainment (publisher of The Sydney Morning Herald, The Age, and the Nine Network), Stan has built a strong catalogue of international content alongside Australian originals.

**Strengths:**
- Strong US studio deals (Sony Pictures, Paramount, NBC Universal content)
- Growing slate of Stan Originals, including Wolf Like Me, Bump, and Spreadsheet
- Sports tier (Stan Sport) covering Super Rugby Pacific, rugby union internationals, and tennis
- Exclusive deals for international content not available on Netflix Australia

**Pricing (approximate):** Basic tier around AUD $10/month, Standard around AUD $14/month, Premium (4K) around AUD $19/month. Stan Sport available as an add-on.

**Geo-restriction:** Stan is geo-restricted to Australia. Content licensing does not extend to New Zealand or elsewhere.

### Binge

Binge is owned by Foxtel (in turn majority-owned by News Corp Australia). It represents Foxtel's streaming play — a standalone app offering much of the premium content that previously required a Foxtel pay TV subscription.

**Strengths:**
- HBO and HBO Max content (Succession, The White Lotus, Game of Thrones, The Last of Us)
- Fox content via Fox Showcase
- A large international film library
- Significantly cheaper than a full Foxtel subscription

**Pricing (approximate):** Basic (SD) around AUD $10/month, Standard (HD) around AUD $18/month, Max (4K HDR) around AUD $22/month.

**Geo-restriction:** Australia only. Australians abroad cannot access Binge without Smart DNS, which is a significant loss given Binge's exclusive HBO content.

### Kayo Sports

Kayo Sports is Australia's leading sports streaming platform, also owned by Foxtel. Its breadth of coverage makes it exceptionally important to Australian sports fans — and its geo-restriction makes it one of the most sought-after services among Australian expats.

**What Kayo covers:**
- AFL (Australian Football League) — all matches
- NRL (National Rugby League) — all matches
- Cricket — Australian home internationals, Big Bash League, WBBL, Sheffield Shield
- Formula 1, MotoGP, NASCAR
- Tennis (Australian Open coverage, ATP/WTA tours)
- NBA, NFL (select coverage)
- Fox Sports general content

**Pricing (approximate):** One screen around AUD $25/month, Two screens around AUD $35/month, Three screens around AUD $45/month. Free trial available.

**Geo-restriction:** Australia only. For Australians abroad, losing Kayo means losing AFL, NRL, and Australian cricket — the three biggest domestic sports.

### 9Now

9Now is the catch-up and live streaming service for the Nine Network, one of Australia's major commercial FTA broadcasters. It carries Nine, 9Gem, 9Go!, 9Life, and 9Rush channels.

**Content:** Reality TV (Married at First Sight, The Block), news (60 Minutes, A Current Affair, Nine News), US acquisitions (The Voice, American Ninja Warrior formats), and the Australian Open (shared rights). The service is free and ad-supported.

**Geo-restriction:** Available only in Australia. Registration is optional for most content but required for some.

### 7Plus

7Plus is the streaming service for Seven Network (Seven, 7mate, 7two, 7flix channels). Free and ad-supported.

**Content:** Sunrise, The Morning Show, Home and Away (a long-running Australian soap opera with a significant international audience), AFL coverage (Seven holds AFL broadcast rights alongside Foxtel/Kayo), and US acquisitions. Home and Away is a particularly significant loss for Australian expats due to its daily broadcast schedule.

**Geo-restriction:** Australia only. Home and Away geo-restriction is a recurring complaint from Australian expats and international fans of the show.

### ABC iview

ABC iview is the streaming service of the Australian Broadcasting Corporation, Australia's public broadcaster (equivalent to the BBC). It is free, publicly funded, and carries the full ABC and ABC Kids content archives.

**Content:** Australian drama, documentary, comedy, children's programming, news, and current affairs. Series like Utopia, Bluey (before its international licensing deals), Q+A, Four Corners, and Australian Story. The ABC's children's content (ABC Kids) is significant for Australian families abroad.

**Geo-restriction:** Australia only. Accessible only from Australian IP addresses.

### SBS On Demand

SBS (Special Broadcasting Service) is Australia's multicultural public broadcaster. SBS On Demand is its free streaming service.

**Content:** International films and series, world cinema, NITV (National Indigenous Television) content, food programming (The Food Channel), and a significant archive of foreign-language content. SBS has exclusive rights to Tour de France and other cycling in Australia. Documentaries are a strength.

**Geo-restriction:** Australia only.

## Free vs Paid Australian Streaming Landscape

Australia has an unusually strong free streaming ecosystem. The four major commercial networks (Nine, Seven, Ten) plus both public broadcasters (ABC, SBS) all offer free ad-supported streaming services with substantial catalogues. This means:

- A household with no paid streaming subscriptions can access a large amount of Australian-made content, US drama, reality TV, news, and documentaries for free
- The free tier's quality is high enough that many Australian households subscribe to only one or two paid services
- The cost of losing access to free Australian streaming abroad is amplified because there is no international paid equivalent — you cannot pay to access 9Now or ABC iview from outside Australia

## New Zealand Streaming Landscape

### TVNZ+

TVNZ+ is the free streaming service of Television New Zealand, the public broadcaster. It carries TVNZ 1, TVNZ 2, and Duke content, plus a growing on-demand library. Content includes US acquisitions, New Zealand drama, news, and reality programming. Free with ads.

### ThreeNow

ThreeNow is the streaming service for Three (owned by Warner Bros. Discovery in NZ). It carries Three and Bravo NZ content, plus an on-demand library. Free with ads.

### Neon

Neon is the subscription VOD service in New Zealand, also owned by Sky NZ (which holds New Zealand Sky satellite pay TV rights). Neon carries HBO content, Sky original series, and international acquisitions — a similar position to Binge in Australia, though the services are operated separately and their catalogues are not identical.

**Pricing (approximate):** NZD $17–22/month depending on tier.

### Sky NZ and Sky Sport Now

Sky NZ's satellite pay TV service is the dominant live sports broadcaster in New Zealand, with rights to cricket (Black Caps, internationals), rugby union (All Blacks, Super Rugby), horse racing, and some football. Sky Sport Now is the standalone streaming version.

## What's Geo-Restricted

The following services are restricted to their home country and unavailable abroad without geo-unblocking:

**Australia only:** Kayo Sports, Stan, Binge, 9Now, 7Plus, ABC iview, SBS On Demand, 10 Play (Ten Network)

**New Zealand only:** TVNZ+, ThreeNow, Neon, Sky Sport Now

**Neither available to each other:** Stan and Neon are separate services in separate markets. Australian and New Zealand Binge/Sky platforms are managed separately. A New Zealander in Australia cannot access TVNZ+ or ThreeNow; an Australian in New Zealand cannot access Stan or Kayo.

---

## Conclusion

Australia and New Zealand have mature, locally distinctive streaming ecosystems with strong free-to-air catch-up services that are unavailable anywhere else. For expats and travellers, the loss of Kayo Sports, ABC iview, TVNZ+, and free FTA catch-up services is significant and has no legal international equivalent. Smart DNS tools provide the practical path to maintaining access to these services from abroad.
