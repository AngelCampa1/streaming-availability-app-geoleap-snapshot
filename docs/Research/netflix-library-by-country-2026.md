# Netflix Library Size by Country 2026

*Published March 2026*

---

## Overview

Netflix operates in nearly every country worldwide, but the content available in each market varies dramatically. These differences stem from a web of licensing agreements, local content regulations, co-production arrangements, and the negotiating power of regional rights holders. Understanding library size disparities helps explain why geo-restriction tools have become so important to international audiences.

The figures below represent approximate library sizes based on publicly available tracking data and third-party aggregators. Exact counts fluctuate as titles are added and removed, and Netflix does not publish official country-by-country totals.

---

## Country-by-Country Library Sizes

### Largest Libraries

**Japan: approximately 6,800–7,200 titles**
Japan consistently ranks among the largest Netflix libraries globally. The combination of locally produced anime, Japanese drama, and strong licensing deals for Western content creates a uniquely broad catalogue. Japan also benefits from Netflix's heavy investment in anime originals — series like Cyberpunk: Edgerunners and Tekken: Bloodline are produced with Japanese studios and remain broadly available.

**South Korea: approximately 6,200–6,800 titles**
South Korea's library has grown substantially following the global success of Korean drama (K-drama) and Korean cinema. Netflix has invested heavily in original Korean productions, and South Korean rights holders have negotiated broad licensing terms. The country's catalogue includes both a deep archive of classic K-dramas and a growing slate of Netflix originals.

**United States: approximately 5,500–6,000 titles**
The US library is often assumed to be the largest, but this has not been true for several years. American studios frequently license content to Netflix for international markets while retaining rights for domestic distribution to competing platforms. The US Netflix library is notably thinner than it was in 2018–2020 as Disney+, Peacock, Max, and Paramount+ pulled content back to their own platforms.

**India: approximately 5,500–6,200 titles**
India's library is large and growing rapidly. Netflix has invested significantly in Hindi, Tamil, Telugu, and regional-language original content. Bollywood licensing is extensive, and the Indian market's size means rights holders have strong incentives to place content here. International content availability is also strong.

**United Kingdom: approximately 5,000–5,500 titles**
The UK library is substantial but more constrained than Japan or South Korea due to strong competition from domestic streamers (BBC iPlayer, ITVX, BritBox) and the complexity of rights splits with existing British broadcasters.

**Canada: approximately 5,200–5,600 titles**
Canada operates in a unique position — close enough to the US market that rights holders often bundle Canadian licences with US deals, but with its own distinct regulatory environment (CRTC Canadian content requirements). Netflix has met local content quotas through original Canadian productions.

**Germany: approximately 4,800–5,400 titles**
Germany's library is mid-size for Europe. Netflix has invested in German originals (Dark, Babylon Berlin co-productions) and has strong licensing deals, but German dubbing requirements and the complexity of Central European rights fragmentation limit some catalogues.

**France: approximately 4,500–5,200 titles**
France has strict local content regulations requiring streaming platforms to invest a percentage of revenue in French productions and to include a minimum proportion of European content. This shapes the library meaningfully — French originals are well-represented, and European co-productions are common.

**Brazil: approximately 4,200–5,000 titles**
Brazil represents Netflix's largest Latin American market. The library includes extensive Portuguese-language content, strong licensing for US studio output, and a growing slate of Brazilian originals. Regional rights fragmentation across Latin America means some content available in Brazil is not available in neighbouring countries.

**Australia: approximately 4,000–4,800 titles**
Australia's Netflix library is moderate in size. Strong competition from local streamers (Stan, Binge) means some content goes elsewhere. Australian Netflix has benefited from Netflix's investment in Australian originals and co-productions with the ABC.

**Spain: approximately 4,500–5,200 titles**
Spain has been a significant production hub for Netflix in Europe, with series like La Casa de Papel (Money Heist) and Elite driving investment. The library is strong in Spanish-language content and European co-productions.

## What Drives Library Differences

**Pre-existing licensing agreements.** Before Netflix expanded internationally, studios and distributors had already sold rights to local broadcasters, pay TV providers, and distributors. When Netflix entered a market, those existing deals prevented it from licensing content already committed elsewhere. These arrangements are unwinding as deals expire, but legacy obligations persist.

**Content windows and holdbacks.** Major studios structure their release windows carefully: theatrical → home video → premium pay TV → streaming. In markets where pay TV is a major revenue driver, studios push back against putting content on Netflix too early or at all.

**Local regulatory requirements.** EU regulations require streaming platforms to include at least 30% European content. Some countries (France, Canada) have additional local production investment requirements. These shape both what Netflix produces and what it can licence.

**Rights territory fragmentation.** A film or TV show's rights may have been sold separately for Germany, Austria, Switzerland, and the German-speaking parts of Belgium. Each deal may have different terms, leaving Netflix with rights in some territories but not others.

**Netflix's own production strategy.** As Netflix's original content slate has grown, it has shifted investment from licensing third-party content toward exclusives and originals. Countries where Netflix has invested most heavily in local originals tend to have richer libraries overall.

## Most Unique Content by Country

Certain countries offer content that is simply unavailable anywhere else on Netflix globally:

- **Japan:** Classic anime series, tokusatsu, domestic game show formats
- **South Korea:** Archive K-dramas from the 1990s–2010s, variety show content
- **India:** Regional-language films in Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam
- **Turkey:** Turkish drama (dizi) series with massive global followings but limited outside Turkey
- **Brazil:** Classic Brazilian telenovelas, comedy specials, regional films
- **France:** Heritage French cinema, Canal+ co-productions not distributed elsewhere

## What Expats Lose When They Move

An expat moving from Japan to the United Kingdom, for example, may find that several hundred to over a thousand titles they watched regularly are simply absent from their new country's Netflix library. Common losses include:

- Anime series licensed for Japan but not for European markets
- Domestic content produced for and exclusive to a specific national market
- Shows whose rights have been sold to competing platforms in the destination country (e.g., a show on Netflix US that is on Disney+ in the UK)
- Films still in pay-TV windows in the destination country

The practical impact varies by viewing habits. Someone who primarily watches English-language Netflix originals (which are available globally) will notice fewer gaps than someone who regularly watches local-language content or niche licensed programming.

## How to Access Larger Libraries

Smart DNS services like GeoLeap allow subscribers to access Netflix as if they were located in another country. By routing the DNS authentication check through servers in a target country (e.g., Japan or South Korea), users can browse and stream from that country's library.

Important considerations:
- Netflix's terms of service state that access should be from the country of the subscription
- Netflix actively detects and blocks many VPN and proxy services
- Smart DNS services that use ISP-grade routing have better detection resistance than datacenter VPNs
- The practice is widespread but operates in a grey area with respect to platform terms

For expats who have legitimate accounts and are simply trying to access content they previously had in their home country, Smart DNS represents a practical and widely-used solution.
