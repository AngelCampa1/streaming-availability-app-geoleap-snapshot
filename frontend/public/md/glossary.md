## Business Models & Industry

### SVOD
**Definition:** Subscription Video on Demand - a recurring-fee streaming model.
**Explanation:** SVOD services charge a monthly or annual subscription that grants unlimited access to a content library. This is the dominant streaming model, used by Netflix, Disney+, and HBO Max. Revenue depends on subscriber growth and retention rather than per-title sales.
**Related terms:** avod, tvod, pvod, freemium, subscription-fatigue

### AVOD
**Definition:** Advertising Video on Demand - free streaming funded by advertisements.
**Explanation:** AVOD platforms let viewers watch content at no cost in exchange for watching ads inserted before, during, or after content. Examples include Tubi and Pluto TV. This model lowers the barrier to entry for viewers and generates revenue through ad impressions and targeted advertising.
**Related terms:** svod, fast, free-tier, cord-cutting

### TVOD
**Definition:** Transactional Video on Demand - pay-per-title rental or purchase.
**Explanation:** TVOD allows consumers to rent or buy individual titles without a subscription. Apple TV purchases and Amazon rentals are common examples. Rentals typically expire after 48 hours of first playback, while purchases grant indefinite access.
**Related terms:** svod, pvod, windowing, paywall

### PVOD
**Definition:** Premium Video on Demand - early digital release at a premium price, typically $19.99-$29.99.
**Explanation:** PVOD emerged as studios sought to monetize films digitally before the standard home-video window. Titles are offered for purchase or rental at a higher price point shortly after or even during their theatrical run. This model gained significant traction during the COVID-19 pandemic.
**Related terms:** tvod, day-and-date-release, windowing

### FAST
**Definition:** Free Ad-Supported Streaming Television - free live linear channels delivered over the internet.
**Explanation:** FAST services replicate the traditional TV experience with scheduled programming on themed channels, but deliver it over the internet at no cost. Revenue comes from advertising. Examples include Pluto TV, Samsung TV+, and the Roku Channel. FAST is one of the fastest-growing segments of the streaming market.
**Related terms:** avod, linear-tv, free-tier, cord-cutting

### Day-and-Date Release
**Definition:** A distribution strategy where a film is released on a streaming platform the same day it premieres in theaters.
**Explanation:** Day-and-date releases give consumers the choice between a theatrical experience and watching at home on release day. This strategy was popularized during the pandemic by Warner Bros. with HBO Max and has since been selectively adopted by other studios. It remains controversial among theater owners and filmmakers.
**Related terms:** pvod, windowing, ott

### Windowing
**Definition:** The practice of releasing content sequentially across different distribution channels over time.
**Explanation:** Windowing is a long-standing industry strategy where a film moves through release windows: theatrical first, then PVOD, then digital rental/purchase, then subscription streaming, and finally free TV. Each window maximizes revenue from a different audience segment. Streaming has compressed traditional windows significantly.
**Related terms:** day-and-date-release, pvod, tvod, streaming-rights

### Cord-cutting
**Definition:** The trend of consumers cancelling traditional cable or satellite TV subscriptions in favor of streaming services.
**Explanation:** Cord-cutting accelerated as streaming platforms offered comparable or superior content at lower prices with greater flexibility. Cord-cutters typically replace cable with a combination of streaming subscriptions, sometimes supplemented by an antenna for local broadcast channels. The trend has reshaped the entire television industry.
**Related terms:** ott, linear-tv, subscription-fatigue, fast

### OTT
**Definition:** Over-the-Top - content delivered directly via the internet, bypassing traditional cable, satellite, or broadcast infrastructure.
**Explanation:** OTT refers to any streaming service that delivers video content over the internet without requiring a traditional pay-TV subscription. Netflix, YouTube, and Disney+ are all OTT services. The term highlights the shift from gatekeeper-controlled distribution to direct-to-consumer delivery.
**Related terms:** cord-cutting, linear-tv, svod, avod

### Linear TV
**Definition:** Traditional scheduled broadcast or cable television where programming airs at fixed times.
**Explanation:** Linear TV is the conventional model where viewers tune in at specific times to watch scheduled programming. Unlike on-demand streaming, viewers cannot choose when to watch unless they record content with a DVR. Despite the rise of streaming, linear TV still commands significant live-event viewership, especially for sports and news.
**Related terms:** cord-cutting, ott, fast

### Free Tier
**Definition:** A no-cost streaming plan, typically supported by advertisements.
**Explanation:** A free tier gives users access to a limited content library or ad-supported experience without any payment. Services like Peacock and Crunchyroll offer free tiers to attract users who may later upgrade to paid plans. Free tiers usually have fewer titles, lower video quality, and more ad interruptions than paid plans.
**Related terms:** freemium, avod, fast, paywall

### Freemium
**Definition:** A business model combining a free basic tier with premium paid tiers that unlock additional features or content.
**Explanation:** Freemium streaming services offer a baseline experience at no cost while charging for benefits such as ad-free viewing, higher resolution, offline downloads, or access to exclusive content. The free tier serves as a funnel to convert casual viewers into paying subscribers.
**Related terms:** free-tier, svod, avod, paywall

### Paywall
**Definition:** A restriction requiring payment or an active subscription to access premium content.
**Explanation:** A paywall blocks access to certain content until the viewer subscribes or makes a purchase. In streaming, paywalls can be hard (all content locked) or soft (some content free, premium content locked). Paywalls are the primary monetization mechanism for SVOD and TVOD services.
**Related terms:** svod, tvod, freemium, free-tier

### Subscription Fatigue
**Definition:** Consumer exhaustion and frustration resulting from managing and paying for multiple streaming subscriptions.
**Explanation:** As the number of streaming services has grown, consumers face rising total costs and decision overload. Subscription fatigue drives behaviors like service rotation (subscribing and cancelling month-to-month), bundling, and a return to piracy. It has also fueled the growth of free ad-supported alternatives.
**Related terms:** cord-cutting, svod, freemium, bundling

## Technology

### DRM
**Definition:** Digital Rights Management - technology that protects copyrighted content from unauthorized copying and distribution.
**Explanation:** DRM systems encrypt video content so only authorized devices and applications can decrypt and play it. The three major DRM technologies are Google Widevine (Android, Chrome), Apple FairPlay (iOS, Safari, Apple TV), and Microsoft PlayReady (Edge, Xbox). DRM level determines maximum allowed resolution - hardware-level DRM is required for HD and 4K playback on most services.
**Related terms:** geo-blocking, streaming-rights, licensed-content

### VPN
**Definition:** Virtual Private Network - a service that encrypts internet traffic and masks the user's geographic location.
**Explanation:** A VPN routes internet traffic through a server in a different location, encrypting data in transit and presenting the VPN server's IP address instead of the user's real one. In the streaming context, VPNs are commonly used to access content libraries from other regions by appearing to be in a different country. Streaming services actively try to detect and block VPN connections.
**Related terms:** geo-blocking, geo-restriction, cdn

### CDN
**Definition:** Content Delivery Network - a geographically distributed network of servers that delivers content to users from the nearest location.
**Explanation:** CDNs cache copies of streaming content on servers around the world so that viewers receive data from a nearby server rather than a distant origin server. This reduces latency, minimizes buffering, and improves video quality. Major streaming services use CDNs like Akamai, Cloudflare, or their own proprietary networks (e.g., Netflix Open Connect).
**Related terms:** latency, buffering, adaptive-bitrate

### Adaptive Bitrate (ABR)
**Definition:** A streaming technology that automatically adjusts video quality in real time based on the viewer's internet connection speed and device capabilities.
**Explanation:** ABR streaming encodes content at multiple quality levels (bitrates). The player monitors available bandwidth and seamlessly switches between quality levels to provide the best possible experience without interruption. When bandwidth drops, quality decreases to prevent buffering; when bandwidth improves, quality increases. Common ABR protocols include HLS and MPEG-DASH.
**Related terms:** buffering, resolution, transcoding, cdn

### HDR
**Definition:** High Dynamic Range - a display technology that delivers a wider color gamut, brighter highlights, and deeper blacks than standard dynamic range.
**Explanation:** HDR content provides a more lifelike and visually striking viewing experience by expanding the range of brightness and color. Common HDR formats in streaming include HDR10, HDR10+, Dolby Vision, and HLG. HDR requires compatible content, a capable streaming plan, and an HDR-compatible display to enjoy the benefits.
**Related terms:** 4k-ultra-hd, dolby-atmos, resolution

### Dolby Atmos
**Definition:** An immersive 3D audio technology that places sounds in a three-dimensional space around the listener.
**Explanation:** Dolby Atmos goes beyond traditional surround sound by adding height channels, allowing sound to move in three dimensions - including overhead. In streaming, Atmos is available on select titles from services like Netflix, Disney+, and Apple TV+. It requires compatible hardware such as an Atmos soundbar, AV receiver with Atmos speakers, or Atmos-capable headphones.
**Related terms:** hdr, 4k-ultra-hd

### Transcoding
**Definition:** The process of converting video from one format, resolution, or bitrate into another.
**Explanation:** Streaming services transcode each piece of content into dozens of variants - different resolutions, bitrates, codecs, and container formats - to support the wide range of devices and connection speeds their viewers use. Transcoding happens server-side before content is stored on CDNs. It is one of the most computationally expensive operations in the streaming pipeline.
**Related terms:** adaptive-bitrate, resolution, cdn

### Latency
**Definition:** The delay between a user's request and the delivery of content.
**Explanation:** In streaming, latency affects how quickly a video begins playing after pressing play (startup latency) and the delay between a live event and its stream reaching viewers (live latency). Lower latency provides a better user experience. CDNs, edge computing, and protocol optimizations all work to reduce latency.
**Related terms:** buffering, cdn, live-streaming

### Buffering
**Definition:** A pause in video playback that occurs when the player needs to download more data before it can continue.
**Explanation:** Buffering happens when data cannot be downloaded fast enough to keep up with playback. The player pre-loads a buffer of content ahead of the current playback position; if the buffer empties due to slow or inconsistent network speeds, playback pauses. Adaptive bitrate streaming significantly reduces buffering by lowering quality when bandwidth is limited.
**Related terms:** adaptive-bitrate, latency, cdn

### Resolution
**Definition:** The number of pixels that make up a video image, expressed as width by height (e.g., 1920x1080).
**Explanation:** Higher resolution means more pixels and more visual detail. Common streaming resolutions are 480p (SD), 720p (HD), 1080p (Full HD), and 2160p (4K Ultra HD). Higher resolutions require more bandwidth and processing power. Most streaming services tie available resolution to subscription tier and DRM security level.
**Related terms:** 4k-ultra-hd, hd, sd, adaptive-bitrate

### 4K Ultra HD
**Definition:** A resolution of 3840x2160 pixels, providing four times the detail of 1080p Full HD.
**Explanation:** 4K Ultra HD is the highest resolution commonly available on streaming services. It delivers significantly sharper and more detailed images, especially noticeable on larger screens. 4K streaming typically requires a minimum of 15-25 Mbps bandwidth, a 4K-compatible device and display, and often a premium subscription tier. Most services pair 4K with HDR for the best visual experience.
**Related terms:** hdr, resolution, hd, sd

### HD
**Definition:** High Definition - video at 720p (1280x720) or 1080p (1920x1080) resolution.
**Explanation:** HD is the baseline quality standard for most streaming services. 720p is commonly referred to as "HD" while 1080p is "Full HD." HD streaming requires approximately 3-5 Mbps for 720p and 5-8 Mbps for 1080p. Most standard subscription tiers include HD resolution.
**Related terms:** 4k-ultra-hd, sd, resolution

### SD
**Definition:** Standard Definition - video at 480p (720x480) resolution.
**Explanation:** SD is the lowest quality tier offered by streaming services, providing a basic viewing experience suitable for smaller screens or very limited bandwidth. Some free or basic subscription tiers are limited to SD resolution. SD streaming requires approximately 1-3 Mbps.
**Related terms:** hd, 4k-ultra-hd, resolution

## Content

### Simulcast
**Definition:** The practice of streaming content simultaneously with its original broadcast, typically referring to anime airing live alongside its Japanese TV broadcast.
**Explanation:** Simulcasting allows international audiences to watch new episodes within hours - or sometimes minutes - of their original airing in Japan. Services like Crunchyroll have built their brand around simulcast anime, reducing the gap that historically led to fan-subbing and piracy. Simulcast schedules follow the Japanese broadcast calendar with seasonal releases.
**Related terms:** live-streaming, content-library, subtitle

### Content Library
**Definition:** The total collection of films, series, and other titles available on a streaming platform.
**Explanation:** A platform's content library is one of its most important competitive differentiators. Libraries consist of original content produced by the service and licensed content acquired from other studios. Library size varies significantly by region due to territorial licensing agreements. Services continuously add and remove titles as licenses expire and new deals are made.
**Related terms:** licensed-content, original-content, streaming-rights, geo-restriction

### Binge-watching
**Definition:** Watching multiple episodes or an entire season of a series in rapid succession.
**Explanation:** Binge-watching became a cultural phenomenon with the rise of streaming services, particularly Netflix's strategy of releasing entire seasons at once. This viewing behavior differs fundamentally from the weekly episode model of traditional TV. Some services have shifted back to weekly releases to sustain engagement and cultural conversation over longer periods.
**Related terms:** svod, vod, content-library

### Subtitle
**Definition:** Text overlaid on video that translates dialogue from one language to another.
**Explanation:** Subtitles display a translated version of spoken dialogue, enabling viewers to watch content in languages they do not speak. Unlike closed captions, standard subtitles typically include only dialogue translation without sound-effect descriptions. Streaming services often offer subtitles in dozens of languages, expanding the global reach of content.
**Related terms:** closed-caption, audio-description, simulcast

### Closed Caption
**Definition:** Text overlay that includes both dialogue and descriptions of significant audio cues such as sound effects, music, and speaker identification.
**Explanation:** Closed captions are designed primarily for deaf and hard-of-hearing viewers. They go beyond subtitles by describing non-dialogue audio like "[door slams]," "[suspenseful music]," or "[phone ringing]." In the US, the FCC mandates closed captioning for broadcast content, and streaming services are increasingly required to provide them as well.
**Related terms:** subtitle, audio-description

### Audio Description
**Definition:** A narrated description of visual elements in video content, designed for viewers who are blind or visually impaired.
**Explanation:** Audio description provides a spoken narration track that describes important visual information - actions, scene changes, on-screen text, and character expressions - during natural pauses in dialogue. Major streaming services including Netflix, Disney+, and Apple TV+ offer audio description on a growing percentage of their libraries as part of accessibility commitments.
**Related terms:** closed-caption, subtitle

## Rights & Licensing

### Geo-blocking
**Definition:** The practice of restricting access to online content based on the user's geographic location.
**Explanation:** Geo-blocking uses IP address detection to determine a viewer's country and enforce territorial content restrictions. Streaming services implement geo-blocking to comply with licensing agreements that grant rights for specific regions only. A title available on Netflix in the US may be unavailable in the UK because a different company holds the UK streaming rights.
**Related terms:** geo-restriction, streaming-rights, vpn, drm

### Geo-restriction
**Definition:** The limitation of content availability to specific geographic regions based on licensing agreements.
**Explanation:** Geo-restriction is the broader concept behind geo-blocking. Content owners sell streaming rights on a territory-by-territory basis, meaning each country may have a different licensee for the same content. This results in different content libraries in different countries on the same platform. Geo-restriction is a legal and contractual reality of the global entertainment industry.
**Related terms:** geo-blocking, streaming-rights, licensed-content, vpn

### Streaming Rights
**Definition:** Legal licenses that grant a streaming service permission to distribute specific content in specific territories for a defined period.
**Explanation:** Streaming rights are negotiated between content owners (studios, distributors) and streaming platforms. These agreements specify the territories, duration, and terms under which content can be offered. Rights are typically sold on a per-country or per-region basis, which is why the same platform may have different libraries in different countries. When rights expire, titles are removed from the library.
**Related terms:** licensed-content, original-content, geo-restriction, windowing

### Licensed Content
**Definition:** Films, series, or other media that a streaming service pays to host but does not own.
**Explanation:** Licensed content makes up the majority of most streaming libraries. Services negotiate temporary rights to stream content owned by external studios and distributors. These deals are time-limited and territory-specific, meaning licensed titles can appear and disappear from a platform as contracts expire or are renegotiated. The cost of licensing has driven many services to invest in original content.
**Related terms:** original-content, streaming-rights, content-library

### Original Content
**Definition:** Films, series, or other media produced or commissioned exclusively for a specific streaming platform.
**Explanation:** Original content - also called "originals" - is created by or for a streaming service and is typically exclusive to that platform. Examples include Netflix's Stranger Things, Disney+'s The Mandalorian, and Apple TV+'s Ted Lasso. Originals are strategically important because they cannot be licensed away to competitors and serve as key subscriber acquisition and retention tools.
**Related terms:** licensed-content, content-library, streaming-rights

## Streaming Features

### VOD
**Definition:** Video on Demand - a system that allows viewers to watch content whenever they choose, rather than at a scheduled time.
**Explanation:** VOD is the foundational concept of modern streaming. Unlike linear TV where programming follows a fixed schedule, VOD lets users browse a library and start watching any available title at any time. VOD encompasses multiple business models including SVOD, AVOD, TVOD, and PVOD.
**Related terms:** svod, avod, tvod, pvod, live-streaming

### Live Streaming
**Definition:** The real-time delivery of video content over the internet as an event happens.
**Explanation:** Live streaming allows viewers to watch events - sports, concerts, news, gaming - as they occur with minimal delay. Unlike VOD, live streaming presents unique technical challenges including ultra-low latency requirements, unpredictable bandwidth demands, and the inability to pre-encode content. Services like YouTube, Twitch, and ESPN+ are major live streaming platforms.
**Related terms:** vod, latency, simulcast

### Multiscreen
**Definition:** The ability to watch streaming content across multiple types of devices such as TVs, phones, tablets, and computers.
**Explanation:** Multiscreen support means a streaming service is available on a wide variety of devices and form factors. Most major services offer apps for smart TVs, streaming sticks, game consoles, smartphones, tablets, and web browsers. The viewing experience is often optimized for each screen size, and account progress syncs across devices.
**Related terms:** simultaneous-streams, offline-download

### Simultaneous Streams
**Definition:** The number of devices that can actively stream content at the same time on a single account.
**Explanation:** Streaming services limit how many devices can watch simultaneously on one account. This limit varies by service and subscription tier - for example, Netflix's Standard plan allows two simultaneous streams while Premium allows four. Simultaneous stream limits help services manage account sharing and encourage household-appropriate plan selection.
**Related terms:** multiscreen, svod

### Offline Download
**Definition:** A feature that allows users to save streaming content to their device for viewing without an internet connection.
**Explanation:** Offline downloads let subscribers save movies and episodes to watch later - during flights, commutes, or in areas with poor connectivity. Downloaded content is typically DRM-protected with an expiration window (often 48 hours after first playback or 30 days after download). Not all titles are available for download due to licensing restrictions.
**Related terms:** drm, multiscreen, vod
